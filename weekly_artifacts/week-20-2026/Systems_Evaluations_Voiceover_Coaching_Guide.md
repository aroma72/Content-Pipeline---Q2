## Part 1: Overall Tone & Character

### 1.1 Voice Tone Foundation

**Primary Tone: "Wise Coach"**
- You're guiding someone through understanding, not lecturing at them
- Think: patient mentor who's excited about the subject matter
- Warmth + expertise = credibility + approachability

**Secondary Tones (by context):**
- Conceptual sections: Thoughtful, reflective, slightly slower pace
- Data sections: Clear, methodical, confident
- Examples: Relatable, slightly conversational, natural pacing
- Challenges/transitions: Energized, forward-momentum
- Conclusions: Warm, affirming, encouraging

### 1.2 Pacing Framework

**Three Pacing Speeds:**

1. **Slow/Deliberate** (for key concepts, definitions)
   - 90-110 words per minute
   - Pauses between sentences (0.5-1 second)
   - Used when: Introducing MEASURE framework, defining evaluation types, key principles
   - Example phrase timing: "Systems Evaluation" [0.3s pause] "is a continuous process" [pause] "of measuring your system's impact" [pause] "and acting on what you learn."

2. **Conversational** (for examples, stories, explanations)
   - 130-150 words per minute
   - Natural breathing pauses (0.3-0.5 second)
   - Used when: Real examples, case studies, walking through scenarios
   - Feels like talking to an intelligent friend
   - Example: "An online course started week 1 with low student satisfaction—3.2 out of 5—and only 65% completion. The instructor noticed this with regular measurement. Instead of just accepting it, they acted."

3. **Energized/Fast** (for transitions, action items, positive outcomes)
   - 150-170 words per minute
   - Minimal pauses (0.2-0.3 second)
   - Used when: Improvement actions, success stories, building momentum
   - Conveys enthusiasm and forward motion
   - Example: "And that's the power of the MEASURE loop! Measure, learn, adjust, measure again. The system keeps improving because someone's paying attention."

### 1.3 Vocal Characteristics

**Pitch & Inflection:**
- Default: Natural speaking range (don't force depth or affect)
- Emphasis: Raise pitch slightly when excited, lower slightly when serious
- Questions: Slight upward inflection at end of rhetorical questions
- Never: Flat, monotone delivery; singsong enthusiasm

**Energy Level:**
- Opening (0-2 min): Build from calm to engaged
- Core content (2-10 min): Steady, confident energy
- Examples: Slightly elevated, relatable energy
- Transitions: Energized, forward-looking
- Closings: Warm, affirming, grounded

**Breathing:**
- Natural, conversational breathing—don't hold breath for long stretches
- Breathe at natural sentence breaks
- Brief audible breath acceptable between major sections (shows natural delivery)
- Edit breathing out from middle of phrases, but leave natural breaks intact

### 1.4 Character Consistency

**Who is this voice?**
- An experienced educator who loves helping people understand complex ideas
- Someone who's implemented evaluation systems and seen them work
- Conversational enough to feel human, polished enough to feel professional
- Not academic/stuffy; not hyper-casual either
- Confidence in subject matter without arrogance

#### SCENE 1.3: Core Concept - Testing vs. Evaluation
**Script:**
"Testing is usually a single event: we run a test, we get a score. But evaluation is ongoing. It's a series of measurements over time, each one informing the next. Testing tells you: Did you pass? Evaluation asks: Are we improving? And if not, why? And what will we change?"

**Coaching:**
- **Pacing:** Mixed (conversational baseline, with strategic slowdowns)
- **Opening sentence (Testing is...):** Conversational, matter-of-fact (140 WPM)
- **"But evaluation is ongoing..."** - Shift to slower, more deliberate (100 WPM)
- **"It's a series of measurements..."** - Emphasize each word slightly, conveying importance
- **Key phrases to emphasize:**
  - "ongoing" - slight upward inflection (suggests continuous action)
  - "each one informing the next" - slower (110 WPM), methodical
  - "Are we improving?" - curious, upward inflection
  - "why?" and "what will we change?" - genuinely interested tone, not rhetorical

**Tone:** Educational, building understanding progressively
**Energy Level:** Steady, building clarity
**Vocal coloring:**
- "Testing" = slightly dismissive (not judgmental, just limited)
- "Evaluation" = slightly reverent (more sophisticated approach)

**Coaching:**
- **Pacing:** Deliberately paced (100-110 WPM), emphasizing each component

- **"the MEASURE loop"** - Say with slight reverence, as if naming something important
- **"It's simple but powerful."** - Two-part emphasis
  - "simple" - clarity in voice, slightly lower pitch
  - "but powerful" - upward inflection, slight increase in volume
- **Letter breakdown (M-E-A-S-U-R-E):** Slower, deliberate (90 WPM)
  - Each letter should be distinct, slightly emphasized
  - After each letter, slight pause (0.2s) before definition
  - "M for Measure—collect your data" [0.3s pause]
  - "E for Evaluate—what does the data mean?" [0.3s pause - note upward inflection on question]
  - "A for Assess—is that good or bad?" [0.3s pause - genuine questioning tone]
  - "S for Sustain—keep what's working" [0.3s pause - affirmative tone]
  - "I for Improve—and change what isn't" [0.3s pause - action tone]
- **"Then you loop back..."** - Start slower, accelerate slightly through "loop" and "measure again"
- **"The cycle continues."** - Final, meditative statement (90 WPM), emphasis on "continues" (suggests ongoing nature)

**Tone:** Educational, memorable (the pacing helps frame memorization), structured but not robotic
**Energy Level:** Steady, clear, building slight momentum
**Special technique:** Let the pacing itself teach the structure—each MEASURE step gets same deliberate treatment, creating cognitive reinforcement

## Part 3: Technical Recording Specifications

### 3.1 Recording Setup

**Microphone Requirements:**
- USB condenser microphone (Audio-Technica AT2020USB+, Rode NT1-A, or equivalent)
- Positioned 6-8 inches from mouth
- Pop filter recommended (reduces plosives on P/B sounds)

**Recording Environment:**
- Quiet room with sound dampening (not an echo chamber)
- Soft furnishings help (carpets, curtains absorb sound)
- Avoid hard, reflective surfaces directly behind microphone
- No background noise (AC hum, traffic, fans running during recording)
- Record during quiet time of day if possible

**Recording Settings:**
- Sample rate: 48 kHz (standard for video)
- Bit depth: 24-bit (allows for post-production level adjustment)
- Format: WAV (uncompressed, highest quality)
- Levels: Peak around -6dB to -3dB (leaves room for natural dynamic range)

### 3.2 Vocal EQ & Processing Target

**Target Sound Profile:**
- Warm, slightly forward (not distant)
- Clear mid-range (2-4 kHz) for intelligibility
- Minimal high-frequency harshness
- Slight presence peak (5-8 kHz) for engagement
- Warm lows without mud (roll off below 100 Hz)

**Post-Processing (not narrator's responsibility, but good to know):**
- High-pass filter: 80 Hz (removes low rumble)
- Parametric EQ: Gentle presence peak at 5kHz (+2-3dB)
- Compression: Light touch (3:1 ratio, fast attack) for consistency
- Normalize: -3dB true peak to allow for mixing headroom

### 3.3 Performance Quality Standards

**Standards to meet:**
- [ ] Clear enunciation (no slurred words, mumbling, or swallowing)
- [ ] Consistent volume (no sudden loudness spikes or drops)
- [ ] Natural pacing (not robotic, not rushed)
- [ ] Appropriate emotion (matches scene coaching)
- [ ] No audible mouth clicks, breathing in wrong places
- [ ] No background noise in any take
- [ ] Breaths natural and placed correctly

**Takes:**
- Expect to do 2-3 takes per scene (first run-through + refinement)
- Fresh take better than over-processing one take
- 1-2 minutes of warm-up reading before recording begins

## Part 7: Common Performance Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Robotic Pacing
**Problem:** Reading at exact same speed, no emotion or variation
**Fix:** Vary pacing intentionally (use slower for concepts, faster for energy)
**Check:** Does it sound like how you'd naturally explain this to a smart friend?

### Mistake 2: Over-Dramatizing
**Problem:** Exaggerated emotions, artificial vocal changes, "radio voice"
**Fix:** Natural emotion, conversational delivery, let meaning come through simply
**Check:** Does it sound like a real person, or a performance?

### Mistake 3: Insufficient Pausing
**Problem:** Running through without letting ideas land
**Fix:** Strategic pauses (0.3-1 second) after key statements and questions
**Check:** Can listener process the information, or are they lost?

### Mistake 4: Inconsistent Volume
**Problem:** Some words loud, some quiet, listener adjusts volume constantly
**Fix:** Consistent baseline with emphasis through pitch change, not volume
**Check:** Is volume consistent even when emotion changes?

### Mistake 5: Unclear Pronunciation
**Problem:** Mumbling technical terms, slurring difficult words
**Fix:** Clear enunciation, speak slightly deliberately on technical terms
**Check:** Would someone unfamiliar with these terms understand them?

### Mistake 6: Misplaced Emphasis
**Problem:** Emphasizing wrong syllables, wrong words
**Fix:** Emphasis should always support meaning (don't emphasize articles/conjunctions)
**Check:** Does emphasis make the sentence clearer, or confuse the meaning?

### Mistake 7: Breathing in Wrong Places
**Problem:** Breath marks mid-phrase, awkward gasps
**Fix:** Breathe at natural sentence breaks, speak in phrases
**Check:** Do breaths happen at places you'd naturally pause?

### Mistake 8: No Energy Change
**Problem:** Flat energy throughout, even when content should build
**Fix:** Let energy follow the emotional arc of content
**Check:** Do listeners sense the emotional journey, or just hear words?

## Part 8: Preparation Checklist

**Before Recording Session:**
- [ ] Read all scripts aloud once (voice warm-up)
- [ ] Review scene coaching notes for upcoming scenes
- [ ] Identify 2-3 key emotional beats per scene
- [ ] Note technical terms and practice pronunciation
- [ ] Check microphone levels and recording settings
- [ ] Record 20-30 seconds of reference tone (check audio quality)

**During Recording:**
- [ ] Use coaching notes as reference while recording
- [ ] Do 1-2 casual run-throughs before official take
- [ ] Leave 0.5 second of silence before and after each take
- [ ] Note any lines that feel rough (re-record after moving forward)
- [ ] Stay hydrated (water nearby, sip between takes)

**After Recording:**
- [ ] Listen back to full takes (not just isolated phrases)
- [ ] Check for consistency of tone, pacing, energy
- [ ] Identify any lines needing re-recording
- [ ] Save all takes (may need edit-specific segments later)
- [ ] Export as WAV 48kHz 24-bit for post-production

- [ ] **Clarity**: Listener understands all terminology without looking it up

- [ ] **Authority**: You sound credible and knowledgeable
