# From My Hands To My Eye — draft

**Teaches:** the shift from specialist to AI orchestrator is an identity change, and the craft moves from your hands to your judgment.
**One move:** pick up the tool only to set the standard, then direct and judge the machines that do the work.
**Real project / everyday spine:** a master tailor whose workshop fills with fast machines, and who has to stop sewing and start directing.

This is an emotional lesson, not a technical one, so it must name the deskilled feeling out loud before
it flips it. The turn is one concrete moment — Ali's eye catches a flaw a machine missed — proving the
craft did not vanish, it moved up a level. The tailor workshop mirrors the engineer's real situation
(the machines are the AI); one closing beat names that link so the learner sees themselves.

This stays clear of the Autonomy Dial module on purpose. It does not teach how much to let the machines
do, how to write their rules, or how to measure them — those are the autonomy videos. This one is only
about the identity move: the craft leaves your hands and lives in your judgment.

Deferred: how to actually run/prompt the machines (the orchestration skills — other modules). How much
autonomy to grant them and how to track it (the Autonomy Dial module). How to score their output with a
rubric (video 5.7, the paired lesson). Team and hiring implications.

## Script (one beat per line · [ali] = character · [info] = infographic)

1.  [info] The hardest part of working with AI is not technical. It is who you become.
2.  [ali]  Ali is the best tailor in town, and he loves every stitch.
3.  [ali]  Each morning he sews one fine coat, slowly, by his own hand.
4.  [ali]  The work feels good in his fingers, and the result is his.
5.  [ali]  Then his shop grows, and fast machines arrive that stitch on their own.
6.  [ali]  Now his job is to run them, not to sew.
7.  [ali]  For a week he feels useless, like his craft was taken from him.
8.  [info] That empty feeling is normal, and almost everyone hits it. *(caption: this feeling is normal)*
9.  [ali]  He almost sells the machines, just to hold a needle again.
10. [ali]  Then a coat comes off a machine with a crooked collar.
11. [ali]  Ali sees it in one second, because his eye is the real skill.
12. [ali]  He threads one seam himself, to show the machine the standard.
13. [ali]  Then he sets that standard for all of them and steps back.
14. [info] His hands made one coat a day. His eye now shapes fifty. *(info: 1 → 50)*
15. [ali]  The stitching was never the craft. The taste was.
16. [ali]  And taste is the one thing the machines still need from him.
17. [ali]  The new work is harder, and honestly it is more interesting.
18. [ali]  He is not a smaller tailor now. He runs the whole cloth.
19. [info] You feel the same the day your AI writes the code you loved writing.
20. [ali]  You did not lose the craft. You moved up to directing it.
21. [ali]  Your turn. Name one thing you still do by hand out of habit.
22. [info] Ask if your value now sits in your judgment, not your hands. *(spoken in full; same words on a card)*
23. [ali]  The needle sets the standard. Your judgment is the job now.

## Gate check

**READY** · 23 beats · 16 [ali] / 7 [info] · average ~11 words per line

- **One move:** move your identity from maker to director-and-judge; use the tool only to set the
  standard. New concepts across the whole script: identity-not-skill, the deskilled feeling, taste-is-
  the-craft, set-standard-then-delegate, the-new-role-is-bigger. Five — under the eight ceiling.
- **Everyday picture:** a master tailor handed fast machines. Fully lived and non-technical; the
  learner feels the loss and the turn without any jargon. Beat 19 ties it to their real work in one line.
- **Before → after:** before is Ali sewing one coat by hand, then feeling useless when the machines
  arrive (3–9). After is Ali catching the flaw, setting the standard, and shaping fifty coats (10–18).
- **Character:** proud specialist → threatened → almost quits → one moment reframes him → runs the
  workshop. A real emotional arc, which is the whole point of this SLO.
- **Close:** name one by-hand habit (21), a verbatim self-question card (22), landing on the new move (23).

**Two emotional checkpoints, by design.** Beat 8 normalises the deskilled feeling ("almost everyone
hits it"); beats 15–18 reassure and reframe. The house standard requires two, and for an identity
lesson they are the load-bearing beats, not decoration. No shaming language anywhere.

**Open question:** I set Ali as a tailor here rather than the shopkeeper of the autonomy/evals series,
because the hand-craft→workshop mirror is the cleanest carrier for identity. If you want one single Ali
world across all modules, say so and I will re-cast this in the shop (harder, but doable).
