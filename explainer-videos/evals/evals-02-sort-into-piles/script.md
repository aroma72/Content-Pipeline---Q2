# Look At Your Data — draft

**Teaches:** find out what is actually going wrong, by reading real bad results and grouping them.
**One move:** sort your bad results into piles, and fix the biggest pile.
**Everyday picture:** the returns basket at Ali's stall. One basket, then four piles on the counter.

Follows video 1. That one ended with a number (seven sweet, three sour). This one starts from what the number cannot tell you.

Deferred: naming it (video 3), writing criteria (video 5), judges (video 6), thresholds (video 7).

## Script (one beat per line · [ali] = character · [info] = infographic)

1.  [info] Sort your bad results into piles, then fix the biggest pile.
2.  [ali]  Last week Ali found that three mangoes in ten were bad.
3.  [ali]  But that number only counted one thing, which was sweetness.
4.  [ali]  It still does not tell him what to change on Monday.
5.  [ali]  So he blames the supplier and orders from someone new, which feels like the obvious move.
6.  [info] The new crate costs more, the van comes later, and three in ten are still bad.
7.  [info] All he has is one basket marked "bad mangoes".
8.  [ali]  The man at the next stall tells him to stop guessing and start looking.
9.  [ali]  So Ali tips the basket out on the counter.
10. [ali]  He picks up the first one. It is green and hard.
11. [ali]  He says it out loud. "Not ripe yet."
12. [ali]  The next one has a dark bruise. "Squashed on the way here."
13. [ali]  He goes through all thirty, naming what is wrong with each one, which is slow but is the part that works.
14. [ali]  Then he pushes them into piles. Same problem, same pile.
15. [info] Not ripe. Bruised. Too small. Sour.
16. [ali]  One pile is much taller than the rest.
17. [info] Bruised nineteen. Not ripe six. Too small three. Sour two.
18. [ali]  Nineteen were bruised. Only two were sour, and sour is what he tested for.
19. [ali]  He goes out to the van and finds the crate under a sack of onions.
20. [info] One basket that told him nothing. Four piles that name the problem.
21. [ali]  So he lines the crate with newspaper.
22. [info] Bruises drop from nineteen to four.
23. [ali]  One cheap fix. He found it by looking, not by guessing.
24. [ali]  Do the same when your AI keeps getting something wrong.
25. [ali]  Collect the bad answers and write what is wrong with each one.
26. [ali]  Then push the same problems together and start with the biggest pile.
27. [info] Or say this to your AI assistant: "Here are twenty bad answers. Write what is wrong with each one, then group them and count each group." *(spoken in full; same words on a card)*
28. [ali]  The biggest pile is your next job.

## Gate check

**READY** · 28 beats · 20 [ali] / 8 [info] · average 10 words per line

- **One move:** note each one, group them, count. One continuous action on one counter.
- **Everyday picture:** the returns basket, beats 9 to 22. No jargon spoken.
- **Before → after:** one unlabelled basket and a more expensive supplier that changed nothing (5–7). Then four named piles, one cause, bruises nineteen down to four (15–22).
- **Character:** pays for the wrong fix, gets told to look, does the work, finds the crate under the onions, fixes it with newspaper.
- **Close:** do it next time (24–26) plus a prompt card (27).

**Beat 18 is the beat that matters.** The sourness he measured in video 1 turns out to be the smallest pile. That is the real lesson without naming it: the thing you already measure is often not the thing hurting you.
