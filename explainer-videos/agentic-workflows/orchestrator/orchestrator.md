# One Lead, Many Hands — draft

**Teaches:** the orchestrator pattern for agentic workflows — one lead agent splits a big task, delegates the parts to worker agents, then combines and checks the results — AND the identity shift it asks of you.
**One move:** for a task too big for one agent, be the lead that plans → delegates → and (the part people miss) combines the pieces and checks they fit.
**Real project / everyday spine:** Ali's sweet shop gets a huge festival order; he stops making every piece himself and becomes the lead who divides it among worker helpers and makes the pieces fit.

Two things run together. The **mechanic** is the orchestrator (split → delegate → synthesize). The
**mental model / emotional spine** — per the Advanced "Mental Models" SLO — is the *identity shift*: the
biggest barrier isn't technical, it's identity. Ali is a craftsman who gets his pride from making each
sweet himself; handing the work out feels like being deskilled ("am I even doing the real work?"). The
reframe, earned at the payoff, is that coordinating the whole order — designing the split, catching the
mismatch, making it all fit — is a **bigger, more interesting role, not a lesser one.** Taught hands-on:
the close is a do-it-now Claude prompt, not a discussion.

Deferred: routing / parallelization / prompt-chaining as separate workflow patterns (each its own video);
evaluator-optimiser loops; how the lead plans the split dynamically beyond a one-line nod.

## Script (one beat per line · [ali] = Ali alone · [scene] = robots/multi · [info] = infographic)

1.  [info]  A task too big for one helper needs a lead, not just more hands.
2.  [scene] A huge festival order lands on Ali's counter one morning.
3.  [ali]   Ali has always been the craftsman who makes every piece himself.
4.  [ali]   He gives the whole order to one helper and waits.
5.  [scene] The helper works all day, falls behind, and starts mixing things up.
6.  [info]  One helper doing everything becomes the bottleneck.
7.  [ali]   So Ali steps back from the bench and becomes the lead instead.
8.  [ali]   At first this feels wrong, like he has stopped doing the real work.  *(emotional checkpoint: names the deskilled feeling)*
9.  [info]  The lead reads the whole order and splits it into parts.  *(orchestrator card: Lead → Sweets · Packing · Notes)*
10. [scene] It hands each part to a worker who is good at that one thing.
11. [scene] One makes the sweets, one packs the boxes, one writes the notes.
12. [info]  The workers each finish their part. What is the lead's most important job now? Write your answer down.  *(QUESTION card: A combine and check they fit · B start a new order · C redo it all itself · D send the workers home — no answer; deliberate hold)*
13. [info]  The lead gathers every finished piece back together.  *(orchestrator card: Sweets · Packing · Notes → Lead)*
14. [scene] It checks the pieces fit, sweets for a hundred and boxes for a hundred.
15. [scene] It catches that the boxes were only fifty, and sends that one part back.
16. [info]  The part people forget is this fitting together, and that is where a lead matters most.  *(mentor reassurance + names: synthesis)*
17. [ali]   Ali sees it now, coordinating the whole order is a bigger job than making one sweet.
18. [info]  One helper alone was slow and mixed up, but a lead with workers is fast and fits.  *(before → after)*
19. [ali]   He is not less of a craftsman, he is the architect of the whole thing.  *(identity reframe)*
20. [info]  The answer is combine the pieces and check they fit.  *(REVEAL card: option A highlighted)*
21. [ali]   Your turn. Take one task too big for a single AI request.
22. [info]  Tell Claude to be the lead, split this into parts, do each, then combine and check them.  *(spoken in full; same words on a card)*
23. [ali]   You are not doing less, you are orchestrating the whole thing.

## Gate check

**READY** · 23 beats · 8 [ali] / 6 [scene] / 9 [info] · average ~12 words per line

- **One concept:** the orchestrator pattern (split → delegate → synthesize/check). The identity shift is the
  emotional framing OF that concept, not a second skill to learn.
- **Everyday picture:** a festival order divided among sweet-shop helpers by a lead; shown, not defined.
- **Before → after:** one helper choking on the whole order (4–5) → a lead + workers, fast and fitting (13–15).
- **Character + identity arc:** craftsman pride (3) → feels deskilled as lead (7–8) → sees the bigger role (17) →
  architect, not less (19) → learner reframe (23). Two emotional checkpoints (8, 16), zero shaming language.
- **Question guardrail honoured:** beat 12 tests ONLY the on-screen orchestrator example (workers finished, what
  does the lead do?) — this video's own concept (synthesis), nothing from elsewhere, nothing not yet on screen.
- **Close is hands-on:** a do-this-now (21) + verbatim Claude prompt (22), landing on the identity line (23).
- **Spoken-form:** every vo line is one-to-two speakable sentences, no colons/semicolons/dashes; numbers spelled.
