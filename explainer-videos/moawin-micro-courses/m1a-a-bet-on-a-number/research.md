## Outside research (how to teach it)

- **Output vs outcome** is the standard product framing of this exact distinction. Teresa Torres,
  Product Talk, 18 Mar 2026: "An output is something you build or produce"; "an outcome is the impact
  of that output, a change in customer behavior". Teams tasked with outputs are "done when the software
  ships"; teams tasked with outcomes are not done until it has the expected impact.
  https://www.producttalk.org/2026/03/outcomes-vs-outputs/
- **The bet framing** (practitioner writing): a hypothesis named as a bet makes the team state the
  stake and accept it can lose; a vague goal becomes a bet through a named metric and target.
  https://tobeproduct.notion.site/Stop-Testing-Hypotheses-start-making-Bets-bb8dcc80f17c4e39a9b644325ee4c81d ·
  https://www.aakashg.com/what-makes-a-good-hypothesis/ (its unsourced "72%" claim is NOT used)
- **IF … THEN … because** template matches ProdPad's: https://www.prodpad.com/glossary/product-hypothesis/
- **Teaching falsifiability:** learners tend to think a hypothesis is something you prove right; the
  fix is to show a case where the data says "no" and treat that as useful (classroom practice, not a
  controlled study). https://sites.miamioh.edu/exemplary-science-teaching/

## Analogy
- **USE: a bet.** A bet names what you expect, by when, and you can lose it. A task can't lose:
  it is "done" the moment it is shipped. (Matches the workbook's own definition, §5.)
- **AVOID: "a hypothesis is a guess."** The workbook wants the reason to come from teachers, not a
  guess (§6.3), so "guess" teaches the wrong thing.

## Misconceptions to pre-empt
1. "If we did the work, the goal is met" (output = outcome) → MOA-8 can't get a verdict.
2. "A good hypothesis is one that comes true" → a bet that loses (null) still teaches (§6.5).
3. "Better engagement" / "improvement" is a number → it isn't; name the RDF metric, from, to, by when.

## Best technique
**Contrast pair, then a worked example on the team's own row:** task vs bet (the workbook's 6%→9%
pair), a real Moawin bet (MOA-9), then the four-part number line built live on MOA-8.

## Discrepancies to flag (not stated as fact in the video)
- **1.67 for observations:** the Scoreboard shows 1.67 → 2.00 as "Sprint 05 baseline → S06 target",
  but §8 also says Sprint 05 actually averaged 1.16 against a target of 1.67. So 1.67 looks like
  Sprint 05's *target*, not its average. The video says "the Scoreboard starts it at 1.67" and does
  not call it Sprint 05's average. Confirm with the data team.
- **MOA-7 typo ("improvemnt")** is quoted exactly as the brief instructs; Aroma to confirm showing it.
