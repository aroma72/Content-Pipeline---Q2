# Video 1 — Stop Opening the Project — draft

**Covers:** guide §1 (the corrected premise)
**Teaches:** an agent you have to open and talk to is a chat window with extra steps; a system that works
on its own needs three things a chat window cannot give it — somewhere to track its work, a two-way line
to you, and a body that is awake when you are not.
**One move:** name which of the three your project is missing today.
**Real project / everyday spine:** Ali opens his project, types the same kind of instruction he typed
yesterday, watches it work, closes the laptop, and nothing about tomorrow is any different.

This video teaches no setup whatsoever — no keys, no scopes, no URLs. Its only job is to replace "I chat
with my agent" with "I ask, and it works without me", so that the four connections in videos 3–7 feel
like four *jobs* rather than four logos. The closing sentence of §1 is the line the whole module builds
toward and it is spoken here in full.

Deferred: the loop itself (video 2), every step (videos 3–7), the mistakes (video 8).

## Script (one beat per line · [ali] = character · [scene] = shop scene · [info] = infographic)

1.  [info]  If you have to open it and talk to it, it is a chat window with extra steps.
2.  [scene] Every morning Ali opens his project and types what he needs.
3.  [scene] It works, and it works well, while he sits and watches it work.
4.  [ali]   Then he closes the laptop, and the agent stops existing.
5.  [ali]   Tomorrow he will open it and type the same kind of thing again.
6.  [info]  That is not an agent doing your work. That is you, doing your work, faster.
7.  [ali]   A system that runs without you needs three things a chat window cannot give it.
8.  [info]  One — somewhere to keep track of what it is doing. *(threepart card, first panel)*
9.  [ali]   Not in its head, and not in a chat history that scrolls away by Friday.
10. [info]  Two — a way to talk to you, in both directions. *(second panel)*
11. [ali]   You tell it what to do. It tells you what happened, or that it is stuck.
12. [info]  Three — a body that is awake when you are not. *(third panel)*
13. [scene] Ali closes the laptop at six, and something somewhere keeps listening.
14. [info]  Four places answer those three needs. *(fourparts — Notion · Slack · GitHub · your deployment)*
15. [ali]   Notion is where the work is tracked. Slack is where the talking happens.
16. [ali]   GitHub is where the work actually lands, as code you can read.
17. [ali]   And whatever you have already deployed is the thing keeping it all awake.
18. [checkpoint] *(nothing on screen — the player pauses, the LMS asks, gives feedback, resumes)*
19. [info]  None of the four is the point. The loop is the point. *(statement)*
20. [scene] Ali asks for something in the shop chat, and goes to serve a customer.
21. [ali]   You stop opening the project and chatting with it.
22. [ali]   You ask in Slack. It works in the background. It tracks itself in Notion.
23. [ali]   And it comes back to you when it is done, or when it is stuck.
24. [ali]   Your turn. Of the three, which one is your project missing today?

## The checkpoint (beat 18)

**Stem.** Your agent answers beautifully every time you open the project and type. Which of the three
things is it missing?

**Options.**
- A — Somewhere to track its work
- B — A two-way line to you
- C — A body that is awake when you are not
- D — All three

**Answer.** D.

**If they get it right.** Exactly — an agent you have to open is missing all three at once, which is why
"connect one thing" never feels like enough. Opening the project *is* the tracking, *is* the
conversation, and *is* the body. Hand each job to something that outlives the session and the agent
starts working without you.

**If they get it wrong.** It is all three, and that is the point of the video. While the only way to
reach it is to open the project: its memory of the task is the chat scrollback, the only conversation is
you typing, and the only thing keeping it alive is your laptop being open. Each of the three needs
something that exists when you are not there — which is exactly what the next seven videos build.

## Gate check

**READY** · 24 beats · 7 [scene] (29%) · 11 [ali] · 5 [info] · 1 [checkpoint] · average ~12 words per line

- **One move:** name the missing one. New concepts: track · talk both ways · stay awake · the four places.
  Four — well under the ceiling, and the same four the module is built on.
- **Leads with the answer:** beat 1 is the thesis, in plain words, before any story.
- **Everyday picture:** a shopkeeper who has to stand over his helper for it to do anything.
- **Before → after:** before is opening and typing every morning (2–5); after is asking in the chat and
  walking away to serve a customer (20). Deliberately not "all four connected" — that is videos 3–7.
- **Emotional checkpoints:** beat 6 names the disappointment without blaming the learner, beat 9 forgives
  the chat-history habit, beat 24 hands it back as one answerable question. Three.
- **Checkpoint:** beat 18, between two spoken beats (17 → 19), both halves authored.
- **Data templates:** threepart built across 8/10/12, fourparts (14), statement (19). Three distinct;
  one plain statement card, under the limit of two.
- **No assignments.** Beat 24 is a spoken reflection prompt.
