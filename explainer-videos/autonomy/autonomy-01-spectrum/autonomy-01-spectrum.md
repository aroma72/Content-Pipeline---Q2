# Not All-Or-Nothing — draft

**Teaches:** every AI task sits on an autonomy spectrum, and you position it per task, not once for the whole agent.
**One move:** set the dial per task by asking one question — "if it goes wrong, can I undo it?"
**Real project / everyday spine:** hiring a new helper at the shop and deciding, task by task, how much to let them do alone.

The all-or-nothing instinct is the whole enemy here, so the video stages both wrong swings before naming
the dial. The 1–10 numbered slider is deliberately **not** introduced — that is video 4. This video only
has to break the switch-thinking and hand over one decisive question. "Can I undo it?" (reversibility) is
the through-line of the whole series and is planted here.

Deferred: the numbered 1–10 slider and moving it by evidence (video 4). Writing the rules that let you set
it higher (video 2). What can go wrong when you hand over (video 3). The full six-level CSA list is a
caption nod, never recited.

## Script (one beat per line · [ali] = character · [info] = infographic)

1.  [info] The first choice with any AI helper is a dial, not a switch.
2.  [ali]  Ali hires a new helper for his shop this morning.
3.  [ali]  His first thought is to hand over everything and go home.
4.  [ali]  By noon it has refunded a customer who never paid.
5.  [ali]  So Ali swings back and lets it do nothing on its own.
6.  [ali]  Now he approves every tiny thing himself, and nothing is faster.
7.  [info] Both ways failed, because he treated it as on or off.
8.  [info] Autonomy is really a dial, from asking first to running alone. *(caption: the autonomy spectrum)*
8a. [info] Which of these could the helper safely run alone? Write your answer down. *(QUESTION card: A read an order back · B give a cash refund · C wire money from the till · D approve a supplier invoice — no answer shown)*
9.  [ali]  At the low end the helper asks him before every action.
10. [ali]  At the high end it runs the whole task while he watches.
11. [ali]  Most tasks belong in the middle, not at either end.
12. [ali]  So the real question is where to set the dial each time.
13. [info] One question sets it. If it goes wrong, can you undo it? *(caption: can I undo it?)*
14. [ali]  Ali lists what the helper might do at his counter.
15. [ali]  Reading back an order is easy to undo, so that runs free.
16. [ali]  Wiring money from the till cannot be undone, so that waits.
17. [info] Easy to undo sits high on the dial. Hard to undo sits low.
18. [ali]  He sets each task on its own, not the helper as a whole.
19. [ali]  Every new task starts low. It earns its way up later.
20. [ali]  This morning's panic is gone, because nothing runs that he cannot catch.
20a. [info] The answer is reading an order back, because it is easy to undo. *(REVEAL card: option A highlighted)*
21. [ali]  Your turn. Pick one task you would give an AI helper.
22. [info] Ask one thing. If it goes wrong, can I undo it in time? *(spoken in full; same words on a card)*
23. [ali]  Answer that, and you have placed your first task on the dial.

## Gate check

**READY (gated 2026-08-07)** · 25 beats · 17 [ali] / 8 [info] · average ~10 words per line  *(added 2 interactive cards: QUESTION 8a, REVEAL 20a)*

- **One move:** position a task on the dial using the undo question. New concepts across the whole script:
  dial-not-switch, low vs high end, per-task, the undo test, start-low. Five — under the eight ceiling.
- **Everyday picture:** hiring a new helper and choosing how much they do alone. Demonstrated (beats 2–6)
  and carried to the close, not stated. A beginner can restate it with zero jargon.
- **Before → after:** before is the two failed swings — hand over all (beats 3–4), hand over nothing
  (5–6). After is the dial set per task, panic gone (14–20). Both visible, both in the shop.
- **Character:** Ali swings wrong twice, feels the cost (a bad refund, a wasted morning), finds the
  question, sorts his own counter tasks, ends calm. He travels; he does not narrate a definition.
- **Close:** pick one task (21) plus a verbatim question card (22), landing back on the dial (23).

**The mistake is the realistic one.** Handing a brand-new helper the whole till, then over-correcting to
zero, is exactly what a nervous first-timer does. The learner recognises themselves.
