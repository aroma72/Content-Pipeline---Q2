# Move The Dial By Evidence — draft

**Teaches:** define an agent's autonomy on a 1–10 slider, and raise it only when a track record earns the next notch.
**One move:** put a number, one to ten, on the task, and move it by evidence instead of by feeling.
**Real project / everyday spine:** a numbered dial Ali draws on the shop wall, moved one notch after a good week.

This is the payoff video: the rule from video 2 and the risk-check from video 3 are what let Ali move the
dial. The whole point is to turn "I trust it more now" from a mood into a number he can defend and reverse.
The 1–10 slider is *named and shown* here for the first time in the series — earned, not announced. The
close is the Claude-bridge prompt.

Deferred: monitoring dashboards and alerting in depth. Multi-agent autonomy. Rolling autonomy back
automatically on a risk signal is shown once (beat 17) but not taught as its own system.

## Script (one beat per line · [ali] = character · [info] = infographic)

1.  [info] Do not raise your trust in an AI by feeling. Move it by a number.
2.  [ali]  Ali knows his helper is doing well, but well is only a feeling.
3.  [ali]  Some days he wants to hand it everything. Some days he takes it all back.
4.  [ali]  A friend asks him a simple question over tea.
5.  [ali]  How much do you trust it, on a one to ten?
6.  [ali]  Ali cannot answer, because he never put a number on it.
7.  [info] So he draws a dial from one to ten on the back wall. *(caption: the Autonomy Slider)*
8.  [info] At one, the helper asks him before every single action.
9.  [ali]  At six, it acts alone inside the rule and shows him after.
10. [info] At ten, it runs the whole task and he only watches.
11. [ali]  His refund task sits at two today, so he marks it there.
11a. [info] After a strong week, where should Ali move the dial? Write your answer down. *(QUESTION card: A all the way to 10 · B up to 6 · C keep it at 2 · D down to 0 — no answer shown)*
12. [ali]  Then he does the thing a feeling cannot do. He counts.
13. [info] Last week the helper handled forty refunds. Thirty nine were right. *(info card: 39 / 40)*
14. [ali]  One number, not a mood, and it earns the helper a notch.
15. [ali]  Ali slides the refund task from two up to six.
16. [ali]  Now it clears small refunds alone, and he reads the log each evening.
17. [ali]  A week later it misreads an order and refunds twice by mistake.
18. [ali]  So the dial moves the other way, back to four for a while.
19. [info] The number climbs on a good week and drops on a bad one.
20. [ali]  Six is as high as this task will ever go, and that is fine.
21. [ali]  Higher was never the goal. The right level was.
21a. [info] The answer is up to six, never to the top, and only on evidence. *(REVEAL card: option B highlighted)*
22. [ali]  Your turn. Put a number, one to ten, on one AI task today.
23. [info] Then tell Claude, "act alone on this only up to level six, and log every action." *(spoken in full; same words on a card)*
24. [ali]  A number you can move beats a feeling you cannot.

## Gate check

**READY (re-gate after edit)** · 26 beats · 15 [ali] / 11 [info] · average ~11 words per line  *(added 2 interactive cards: QUESTION 11a, REVEAL 21a)*

- **One move:** put a 1–10 number on the task and move it by evidence. New concepts across the whole script:
  the slider, its anchor points, evidence-moves-it, it-moves-both-ways, right-level-not-highest. Five —
  under the ceiling, and the anchors are one card, not five lessons.
- **Everyday picture:** a numbered dial on the wall and a week's tally of refunds. Both drawable and lived;
  the "how much, one to ten?" question is something anyone can answer about a person.
- **Before → after:** before is trust as a feeling — Ali can't answer the one-to-ten question (beats 2–6).
  After is the number doing the work — 39 of 40 pushes the dial 2→6, and one bad week pulls it to 4 (13–18).
- **Character:** Ali is stuck swinging between all and nothing, gets asked a question he can't answer,
  builds the dial, counts, moves it up on evidence and back down on a mistake, and accepts a ceiling.
- **Close:** put a number on one task (22), a verbatim Claude prompt (23), landing on number-beats-feeling
  (24), which ties back to beat 1.

**The dial moves both ways on purpose (beats 17–18).** The audited failure mode is treating autonomy as a
one-way ratchet to "maximum." Showing it drop after one bad week is what makes it a real instrument and
kills the "higher is better" misconception.

**Open question:** I used 39 of 40 as the evidence number and a 2→6 move, matching the series continuity
sheet. If you want the tutorial-style close (a second on-screen prompt showing how to ask Claude for the
weekly count), I can add one beat, but it nudges the concept budget.
