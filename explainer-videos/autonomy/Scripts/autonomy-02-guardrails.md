# The Rule That Lets Go — draft

**Teaches:** guardrails are not restrictions; a guardrail is what lets you safely raise an agent's autonomy.
**One move:** write one rule so the helper can act on its own, instead of asking you every time.
**Real project / everyday spine:** the shop's refund rule — "under five hundred, go ahead; five hundred or more, ask Ali."

The reframe is the whole battle: learners hear "guardrail" and picture a cage that makes the AI do *less*.
So the video shows the opposite in the shop — one rule buys Ali his afternoon back. The mountain-road
barrier carries the idea in one image (the rail is why you can take the bend fast). The three *kinds* of
guardrail appear only as a closing one-card map, because teaching a taxonomy as the spine is a gate failure.

Deferred: the three guardrail types in depth (each could be its own follow-up). What can still go wrong past
the rule (video 3). Putting a number on the autonomy the rule unlocks (video 4).

## Script (one beat per line · [ali] = character · [info] = infographic)

1.  [info] A good rule is not a cage. It is what lets you walk away.
2.  [ali]  Ali set his helper low, so it asks before every refund.
3.  [ali]  All morning he leaves the shelves to approve fifty rupees here, two hundred there.
4.  [ali]  By lunch he has approved thirty refunds and done none of his own work.
5.  [ali]  He thinks any rule would just make the helper do less.
6.  [ali]  So he writes none, and stays chained to the counter.
6a. [info] Which refund should the helper handle without asking Ali? Write your answer down. *(QUESTION card: A a 200 rupee refund · B a 500 rupee refund · C a 3000 rupee refund · D none of them — no answer shown)*
7.  [info] But a rule is like the barrier on a mountain road.
8.  [info] The barrier is the reason you can take the bend at speed.
9.  [ali]  So Ali stops, and writes his helper one line instead.
10. [info] "Refunds under five hundred, go ahead. Five hundred or more, ask Ali." *(spoken in full; same words on a card)*
11. [ali]  A customer wants two hundred back for a torn bag.
12. [ali]  The helper checks the rule, refunds it, and logs it. Ali never looks up.
13. [ali]  Another wants three thousand, and the helper stops and asks him.
14. [ali]  For the first time all day, Ali is restocking while refunds still happen.
15. [info] One rule turned "ask me every time" into "go ahead".
16. [ali]  The rule did not weaken the helper. It let Ali step back.
17. [info] Rules like this come in three kinds, so you cover each one.
18. [info] What it may touch. When it must stop and ask. What it must refuse. *(caption: permissions / checkpoint / input)*
19. [ali]  Ali needed only the first two today, and that was enough.
19a. [info] The answer is the two hundred rupee refund, because it is under the limit. *(REVEAL card: option A highlighted)*
20. [ali]  Your turn. Take one thing your AI keeps asking you to approve.
21. [info] Write the line. Under this limit go ahead, over it come to me. *(spoken in full; same words on a card)*
22. [ali]  One rule, and you get your afternoon back.

## Gate check

**READY (re-gate after edit)** · 24 beats · 13 [ali] / 11 [info] · average ~10 words per line  *(added 2 interactive cards: QUESTION 6a, REVEAL 19a)*

- **One move:** write one rule that lets the helper act alone. New concepts across the whole script:
  guardrail-enables-autonomy, the cap, the stop-and-ask checkpoint, and a three-kinds map. Four core, and
  the map is a nod not a lesson — under the eight ceiling.
- **Everyday picture:** the refund rule, shown working (beats 11–14), plus the mountain-road barrier as the
  reframe image (7–8). Both are lived and drawable; neither is a verbal-only metaphor.
- **Before → after:** before is Ali approving thirty refunds and doing no work (beats 2–4). After is the
  helper clearing a 200-rupee refund alone while Ali restocks (11–14). The value is *seen*, not asserted.
- **Character:** Ali is chained to the counter, resists the idea of a rule, writes one line, and physically
  walks away to the shelves while work still gets done. Real arc, real relief.
- **Close:** pick the approval you're tired of (20) plus a verbatim rule card (21), landing on "your
  afternoon back" (22).

**The three-kinds card is a map, not the lesson.** Beat 18 names them once so the SLO's "three guardrail
types" is honoured, but the spine is the single refund rule taught in depth. Teaching all three as equals
would be the exact taxonomy-overload the house standard forbids.

**Open question:** the SLO literally says "three guardrail types." I taught one deeply and mapped the three
on one card. If the assessment needs each type demonstrated, that is a **second video**, not more beats
here. Flagging so we don't overload this one.
