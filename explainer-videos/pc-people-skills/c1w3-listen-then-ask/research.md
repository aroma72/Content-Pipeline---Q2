# Research: Listen first, then ask (C1 W3, P&C Buddies)

## Verified facts

1. **RASA = Receive, Appreciate, Summarise, Ask.** Julian Treasure, "5 ways to listen better", TEDGlobal, July 2011. "Rasa" is Sanskrit for "juice" or "essence". Appreciate means the small sounds ("hmm", "oh", "OK"). For Summarise he says "the word 'so' is very important". Ask means asking questions *afterwards*. It is tip 5 of 5. https://www.ted.com/talks/julian_treasure_5_ways_to_listen_better?view=transcript (cross-checked against the transcript at english-video.net/v/en/1200).
2. In the same talk Treasure says we spend "roughly 60 percent of our communication time listening" but "retain just 25 percent" of what we hear. These are his figures and he gives no study for them, so say "Julian Treasure estimates". Do not present them as research.
3. **OARS = Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries.** These are the core skills of Motivational Interviewing (Miller & Rollnick, *Motivational Interviewing*, 3rd ed., 2013). https://psychwire.com/free-resources/research/resource-1ge1hbi/oars-and-motivational-interviewing · https://positivepsychology.com/motivational-interviewing-exercises/
4. **The MI fidelity code expects more reflections than questions.** In MITI, a reflection-to-question ratio of 1:1 counts as fair and 2:1 counts as good. https://casaa.unm.edu/assets/docs/miti1.pdf · https://motivationalinterviewing.org/sites/default/files/MITI%203.1.pdf
5. **The "righting reflex"** is MI's name for the urge to fix someone's problem straight away. It tends to produce resistance ("sustain talk"). https://trainingwithdrellen.com/2017/12/14/the-righting-reflex-in-motivational-interviewing/
6. **The Question Funnel runs open, then probing, then closed.** Open questions start the conversation, probing questions dig into what came up, and closed questions confirm the details at the end. https://www.revolutionlearning.co.uk/article/the-questioning-funnel/ · https://www.callcentrehelper.com/funnel-questions-with-examples-201769.htm. (The MindTools page orders it differently. The open-to-closed order is the common one and is the one to teach.)
7. **Leading questions** build an assumption into the question, for example "How late will the project be?" MindTools warns that using them for your own ends is manipulative. https://www.mindtools.com/a2baqhc/questioning-techniques/
8. **"Why did you…?" tends to sound like blame.** Coaching guidance recommends "What led you to…?" instead. https://hr.umn.edu/supervising/resources/Asking-Powerful-Questions-0 · https://sunlight.academy/how-to-ask-more-than-why-questions-in-coaching/
9. **Listening can change people more than feedback does.** Feedback tends to make people defensive, while being listened to makes them less defensive and more open. Itzchakov & Kluger, HBR, 17 May 2018. https://hbr.org/2018/05/the-power-of-listening-in-helping-people-change. Listening can also be trained: a longitudinal study found that teachers who received more listening training reported more psychological safety (Wiley, *Psychology in the Schools*). https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/pits.22835

## Steps with one Buddy phrase each

**RASA (the listening loop)**
- Receive: put the phone face down, turn towards them, and say nothing.
- Appreciate: "Hmm… achha… go on."
- Summarise: "So what I'm hearing is the deadlines aren't the problem. Not being asked is."
- Ask: "What would help most right now?"

**OARS (for reference only; see the recommendation below)**
- Open: "How has this month been for you?"
- Affirm: "It took courage to raise this."
- Reflect: "You sound tired of chasing approvals."
- Summarise: "So, three things: …"

**Question Funnel (the order of the Ask)**
- Open: "Tell me about the new team."
- Probing: "You said 'left out'. What does that look like day to day?"
- Closed: "So it's mainly the Monday planning meeting. Is that right?"

## Recommendation: how to combine them

Teach **RASA as the spine** and put **the Funnel inside its A**. RASA gives the order of the whole conversation (listen before you ask). The Funnel gives the order of the questions themselves (open, then probing, then closed).

**Drop OARS as a named model.** It overlaps RASA almost one for one: Reflections and Summaries map to Summarise, Open questions map to Ask, and Affirmations sit close to Appreciate. Two four-letter acronyms in about 2 minutes would be one too many. Instead, keep two OARS ideas as single lines: *reflect before you question* (the 2:1 idea) and *resist the urge to fix* (the righting reflex). Name OARS only in the assignment or the instructor pack.

## Misconceptions to pre-empt

1. **"Listening means staying silent."** Appreciate and Summarise are both active. Silence with no "so…" can feel like you aren't interested.
2. **"Good questions = more questions."** Too many questions in a row feels like an interrogation. Reflect first, then ask one question.
3. **"I'm helping by giving the solution quickly."** That is the righting reflex. The person stops talking before they get to the real issue.

## Analogy

**Best: a doctor's consultation.** A doctor listens to the symptoms first, then asks broad questions, then narrow ones, and only then writes a prescription. A Buddy who prescribes in the first minute treats the wrong illness. This fits both the Funnel and "solve too early".

**Avoid:** "listening like a tape recorder" (it is passive and contradicts Appreciate) and "detective / interrogation" (it suggests suspicion, the opposite of the Buddy role).

## Three workplace situations (Ali is the Buddy, per house standard; the others are named by role and given no pronouns)

**1. "Can we have a quick chat?"** A field coordinator stops Ali by the chai station and says the new travel form is confusing.
- *Bad:* Ali explains the form in 30 seconds. "Easy, just fill section B!" The coordinator nods and leaves.
- *Good:* Ali gives full attention (Receive), says "Hmm, go on" (Appreciate), then "So it's not only the form?" (Summarise). The coordinator admits that reimbursements have been late for three months and the rent is due. Ali then works down the Funnel: "What's been happening with the claims?" (open), "Which months are pending?" (probing), "Shall I check with Finance by Thursday?" (closed).

**2. Exit interview: leading questions vs open questions.** A content designer is leaving after two years.
- *Bad:* "You're leaving for a better salary, right? And the team was supportive, wasn't it?" Both answers are yes, so nothing is learned.
- *Good:* "What made you start looking?" The answer: "Honestly, I stopped growing after my lead left." Ali replies: "So growth stopped, and that mattered more than pay." Then: "What would have made you stay?" Then: "Would you be OK with us sharing that with leadership anonymously?"

**3. A colleague returning from leave seems quiet in team meetings.**
- *Bad:* "Why aren't you speaking up anymore?" This sounds like blame, and the colleague gets defensive.
- *Good:* Ali says: "I've noticed you've been quieter since you got back. How is settling in going?" Ali listens, reflects "So the team changed its tools while you were away, and you feel behind", and then asks "Which tool would help most to learn first?"
