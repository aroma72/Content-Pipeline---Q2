# Research — C1W4 "Warm but fair: empathy while staying objective"

## Verified facts

1. **Ladder of Inference origin:** devised by Chris Argyris (Harvard organisational psychologist), popularised by Peter Senge et al. in *The Fifth Discipline Fieldbook* (1994; Rick Ross wrote the ladder chapter). — https://www.toolshero.com/decision-making/ladder-of-inference/ ; https://nrfirescience.org/resource/15842 (accessed 2026-10-07)
2. **Rungs (bottom → top):** observable data → selected data → added meanings → assumptions → conclusions → beliefs → actions. — https://asana.com/resources/ladder-of-inference ; https://www.tempo.io/blog/ladder-of-inference (2026-10-07)
3. **Reflexive loop:** our beliefs decide which data we notice next time, so a label ("careless") keeps confirming itself. — Fieldbook summary, https://cdn.edreports.org/legacy/files/Ladder-of-Inference-OCDE.pdf (2026-10-07)
4. **Climb-down questions** in the Fieldbook include "What is the observable data behind that statement?" and "Can you run me through your reasoning?" — same source; corroborated by https://healthydemocracy.org/wp-content/uploads/The-Ladder-of-Inference_short-example-and-summary.pdf (2026-10-07)
5. **GROW origin:** developed in the UK in the late 1980s by Graham Alexander, Alan Fine and Sir John Whitmore; made famous by Whitmore's *Coaching for Performance* (1992). Whitmore credits Max Landsberg with the acronym. — https://www.resources.hellomonday.co/the-grow-model-for-coaching-origins-and-application-sir-john-whitmore/ ; https://www.thecoachingtoolscompany.com/the-grow-model-explained-for-coaches-questions-tips-more/ (2026-10-07)
6. **GROW = Goal, Reality, Options, Way forward** (Whitmore's books use "Will"; both are common). — same two sources.
7. **Empathy vs sympathy:** Brené Brown: "empathy fuels connection, sympathy drives disconnection"; an empathic reply rarely starts with "At least…". — https://www.psychologytoday.com/nz/blog/partnering-in-mental-health/201408/bren-brown-empathy-vs-sympathy-0 (2026-10-07)
8. **Brown's four qualities come from nursing scholar Theresa Wiseman** ("A concept analysis of empathy", *J. Advanced Nursing* 23, 1996): see the world as they see it, stay out of judgement, understand their feelings, communicate that understanding. — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1046/j.1365-2648.1996.12213.x (2026-10-07). *Note: "staying out of judgement" is the bridge — empathy itself requires not taking sides.*
9. **Neutral ≠ uncaring (HR practice):** Acas guidance says keep an open mind, avoid prejudging, consider evidence for and against, and look after the wellbeing of everyone involved (signpost EAP / support). — https://www.acas.org.uk/investigations-for-discipline-and-grievance-step-by-step/step-2-preparing-for-an-investigation (2026-10-07)

## The Ladder in plain words
1. **What happened** (what a camera would record) 2. **What I noticed** 3. **What I think it means** 4. **What I assumed** 5. **What I concluded** 6. **What I now believe** 7. **What I do**.

**Climb-down questions** (for the Buddy, or to ask the person reporting):
- "What exactly did you see or hear — dates, times, words?"
- "What else was happening that we haven't looked at?"
- "Is there another explanation that fits the same facts?"

## GROW — one Buddy question per stage
- **Goal:** "What would you like to be different by the end of this month?"
- **Reality:** "What's happening right now — what have you already tried?"
- **Options:** "What are two or three things that could help — including things within policy?"
- **Way forward / Will:** "What's one step you'll take this week, and when shall we check in?"

The Buddy asks; the employee chooses. GROW keeps the Buddy out of rescuing and promising.

## Misconceptions to pre-empt
1. **Empathy = agreeing.** No — "I understand why you feel that" is not "you're right." You can validate a feeling without endorsing a version of events.
2. **Neutral = cold.** Fairness is a kindness: both sides are heard, nobody is prejudged. Warmth is in tone; fairness is in process.
3. **Helping = fixing / promising.** Saying "I'll make sure you get leave" feels kind but over-promises; outcomes belong to policy and the line manager.

## Analogy
- **Best:** *A referee who still checks on an injured player.* Cares about every player, but calls the game on what actually happened (the replay = bottom rung). Simple, visual, culturally familiar (cricket umpire works too: "the umpire checks the replay before giving out").
- **Avoid:** "Be a judge" (implies verdicts — Buddies don't decide) and "be a friend/shoulder to cry on" (pulls toward sympathy and side-taking).

## Worked situations (Pakistani office, invented gender-neutral people)

**1. "Ali is always late and doesn't care."** A team lead tells the Buddy this in the Islamabad office.
- *Wrong:* "Yes, I've noticed too — I'll have a word with Ali." (Buddy jumps to the top rung, takes a side.)
- *Right:* climb down — "Which days, what times?" → Data: late 3 times in two weeks, all Mondays, ~20 min. Meaning "doesn't care" is an inference. Buddy then asks Ali openly: "I noticed Mondays have been tricky — what's going on?" (Turns out: a new van route from Rawalpindi.) Conclusion now rests on facts.

**2. Personal crisis.** Ali's parent is in hospital in Lahore; Ali is tearful and missing deadlines.
- *Wrong (sympathy + over-promise):* "Oh no, at least it's not serious! Don't worry, I'll get you two weeks off."
- *Right (empathy + GROW):* "That sounds really heavy. Thank you for telling me." G: "What matters most for you this month?" R: "What's already in place at work?" O: "Options could be compassionate leave, a short work-from-home spell, or shifting a deadline — your manager approves those; I can help you ask." W: "Shall we draft the message to your manager together today?" Signpost support; promise only what the Buddy controls.

**3. Two-sided conflict.** Ali says a colleague, Noor, "takes credit in meetings"; Noor later tells the Buddy Ali "never shares updates."
- *Wrong:* nodding along with whoever is in the room ("Honestly, that's so unfair to you").
- *Right:* "I can hear this has been frustrating" (empathy) + "Can you give me a specific meeting and what was said?" (ladder) to each, separately; no verdict on the spot; same questions, same tone for both; agree next step (facilitated conversation via line manager/HR).

## Pedagogy notes
- Teach the ladder as a **story told twice**: once climbing up fast (the rumour), once climbing down slowly (the facts). Learners remember the contrast.
- Show the rungs as a literal visual ladder; name only 4 rungs on screen if time is tight (Facts → Meaning → Conclusion → Action) and keep the full 7 in the guide.
- Pair each model with one sentence-starter the Buddy can say tomorrow.
