# The question for c1w4-warm-but-fair

This question is **not in the video**. The LMS pauses the video at the point below, shows it as a
pop-up, the learner must pick an answer to continue, sees the feedback, and the video resumes.
The LMS developer reads it from `checkpoint.json` in the repo. To change anything here, tell Claude:
the edit goes into the checkpoint in beats.js and both files are regenerated.

## Question 1 — pauses at 1:26 of the final video

*After the line in beat 13; resumes on beat 15.*

**An employee whose parent is in hospital asks you to guarantee them two weeks off. What do you say?**

A. "Done. I'll make sure you get the two weeks."
B. "Leave isn't my department, you'll need to ask your manager."
C. "Let's look at the options together, and I'll help you ask your manager today."  ✅ *correct*
D. "At least it's only two weeks you need."

**If they choose the right answer, they see:**

> Exactly. You stay warm and on their side of the problem, while being honest that the decision sits with their manager. Helping them ask today is support you can actually deliver.

**If they choose a wrong answer, they see:**

> The fair and warm answer is to explore the options together and help them ask their manager today. Promising the two weeks feels kind, but leave is the manager's decision, so it is a promise you may not be able to keep, and it is unfair to others who follow the process. Sending them off to their manager is accurate but cold: it leaves them alone at a hard moment. "At least…" is sympathy, not empathy; it shrinks what they are going through.
