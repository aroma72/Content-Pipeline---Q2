# Works Isn't the Same as Good — draft

**Teaches:** how to evaluate an AI agent's output quality with a structured rubric of named dimensions.
**One move:** judge the output on five named checks, not just "did it work", so you know exactly which one broke.
**Real project / everyday spine:** a tailor whose machines make coats that "work" but keep failing in ways one glance never asked about.

De-duplication note (this is deliberate). The Autonomy Dial module already teaches "replace a feeling with
a number" and "move trust by evidence" (video 4). This video does **not** re-teach that. It assumes the
learner already checks the work, and teaches the next thing only: that a single "does it work" verdict
hides *where* the work is weak, so you break quality into **named dimensions** and learn which one failed.
The word "score" is used lightly; the lesson is the **named checks**, not the number.

Deferred: writing a good rubric line and how an LLM-as-a-judge runs it — the evals series already teaches
both (video-05-write-the-rubric, video-06-llm-as-a-judge); beat 21 points there, it does not re-teach.
How much autonomy to grant the agent (Autonomy Dial module). Baselines and regression (evals video 7).

## Script (one beat per line · [ali] = character · [info] = infographic)

1.  [info] Your AI's work can pass "does it work" and still be bad.
2.  [ali]  Ali's machines finish a coat, and it works. It fits and looks right.
3.  [ali]  Like most of us, he asks it just one thing. Does it hold together?
4.  [ali]  That is the easy question, and it feels like enough. It is not.
5.  [ali]  The returns start, each for a reason that one question never asked.
6.  [ali]  One coat used twice the cloth and ate a whole day's profit.
7.  [ali]  One had a seam that ripped the first time a man reached up.
8.  [ali]  One was missing an inner button, unnoticed until winter.
9.  [ali]  Every coat worked. Every coat was still not good.
10. [info] "Works" is one question. "Good" is more than one.
11. [ali]  So Ali writes the real questions on a card above his table.
12. [info] Complete. Correct. Not wasteful. Safe to wear. Cleanly finished. *(caption: completeness · accuracy · efficiency · safety · style)*
13. [ali]  Now each coat has to meet all five, not just the first.
14. [ali]  The double-cloth coat fails "not wasteful", and he catches it early.
15. [ali]  The weak seam fails "safe to wear", long before a customer does.
16. [info] Naming the five tells him which one broke, so he knows what to fix.
17. [ali]  He points the machine at the failed line, and it mends just that.
18. [ali]  The same five fit almost anything your AI hands back to you.
19. [info] Complete, correct, efficient, safe, and cleanly finished, whatever the task. *(caption: the five hold for any output)*
20. [ali]  And five is enough. You do not need a longer list to start.
21. [ali]  Later a helper can score the five for you, the way you would. *(caption: see the LLM-as-a-judge lesson)*
22. [ali]  Your turn. Take the last thing your AI built for you.
23. [info] List the five things that make it good, then check each one. *(spoken in full; same words on a card)*
24. [ali]  "It works" is where you start, not where you stop.

## Gate check

**READY** · 24 beats · 15 [ali] / 9 [info] · average ~10 words per line

- **One move:** judge output on five named checks, not one "does it work". New concepts across the whole
  script: works-is-not-good, the five named dimensions (one card), naming shows what-broke-and-to-fix,
  the five reuse on any output. Four — under the eight ceiling; the five dims are a card, not five lessons.
- **Everyday picture:** coats that "work" but waste cloth, rip, or miss a button. Lived and drawable; a
  beginner can picture each failure and explain it back.
- **Before → after:** before is the single "does it hold together" that ships three flawed coats (2–9).
  After is the five-line card catching each flaw and pointing at the exact fix (13–17).
- **Character:** Ali trusts the easy question, eats the returns, writes the five, and now fixes by line.
  Continues his 5.4 arc from maker to judge, without repeating it.
- **Close:** pick one AI output (22), a verbatim five-checks card (23), landing on "works is where you
  start" (24).

**What makes this NOT the autonomy slider video.** Video 4 taught *measure instead of feel*. This one
takes measuring as given and teaches *what to measure* — five named dimensions — and, crucially, that a
single verdict hides the failure location. The payload is beat 16: naming the five tells you which one
broke. No trust dial, no evidence tally, no "number beats feeling".

**Two emotional checkpoints.** Beat 4 normalises asking only "does it work" ("like most of us... feels
like enough"); beat 20 reassures ("five is enough, you do not need a longer list"). No shaming language.

**Open question:** "safe to wear" is output quality (the seam holds), which is a *different* sense of
"safe" than the Autonomy Dial risk video (an action you cannot undo). If that collision worries you, I
can rename this dimension "sturdy" on screen while keeping "safety" as the underlying rubric term.
