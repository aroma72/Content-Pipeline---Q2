2 · The six questions

These cost nothing to answer and should not have waited.

2.1 · Does the playable copy include the 2.6-second intro? (your items 9, §2.10)

atSeconds already includes it. Do not add it yourself. It is measured from the start of <videoId>_final.mp4 — the file we serve and the file we upload. lessonAtSeconds excludes it, and exists only so the two can never be silently confused.

Measured, not asserted. There is now a test that probes the real bumper and the real deliverable on every run:

intro.mp4                       2.600 s   (ffmpeg, measured)
bumper-durations.json           2.600 s   (the constant the container uses)
atSeconds - lessonAtSeconds     2.600 s   (every checkpoint, every video)
Read timing.introOffsetSource. Never assume 2.6.
probed — measured from that video's own intro. brand-constant — the committed measurement, and the usual answer in production, where .mp4 files are not in the image. no-bumpers — delivered bare, so the offset is genuinely 0 and atSeconds === lessonAtSeconds; adding 2.6 to one of these fires every checkpoint 2.6 s late. assumed — and that is exactly the case where timing.trusted is false.

2.2 · Are slugs immutable? (your item 13)

Yes. What churned was existence, not identity. A slug is minted once, before the video is made, and is never rewritten. We went and checked …-r2fi specifically.

Here is what actually happened. Videos made through /demo/make-video are written into the container's filesystem at runtime, and our image build strips out/, art/ and every .mp4. Exactly one such folder is committed to our repository. So a redeploy took them, and the slug was never touched. Because a re-run of the same topic draws a fresh timestamp suffix, a lesson re-made afterwards reappears under a new videoId — which from where you are standing is indistinguishable from a rename.

You should not have had to work that out from the outside, and you should not have to again. Every catalogue row now says so itself:

"durability": "committed"   in our repository; survives a redeploy. Safe to link.
"durability": "ephemeral"   made at runtime; WILL disappear. Do not link a block to it.
"durability": "unknown"     we could not tell. Treat as ephemeral.

Of the 24 rows in the catalogue today, 23 are committed. We would rather tell you unknown than guess: a row wrongly called durable is a dead question inside one of your courses, and a learner finds it before either of us does.

2.4 · Is timingTrusted: false transient, and can it flip after linking? (item 12)

It can flip in both directions, and yes, after you have linked. Two independent causes, and they behave differently:

  • A missing beat length. durations.json is written by the voiceover step; until it exists, or if any non-checkpoint beat is missing from it, every later time is a guess. This is transient — it becomes true when the video is next rendered.
  • The intro offset was guessed (introOffsetSource: "assumed"), meaning we could neither probe the bumper nor read the committed constant. Rarer, and a misconfiguration on our side rather than a stage not yet run.

Your three untrusted videos changing identity between Tuesday and Wednesday is the first cause: those are videos mid-pipeline, not videos that broke. Treat it as a live field, not a property of the link. Re-read it when you serve, and refuse to fire a checkpoint whose timing.trusted is false at that moment, rather than at the moment you mapped it.

2.5 · catalogueUpdatedAt, and is the ETag payload-derived? (item 14)

The ETag is payload-derived — a SHA-1 of the exact serialised body — so it is stable while the content is and changes only when something real does. Two cautions, because we would rather you hear them from us than discover them at 100 rows:

  • It is compared as a raw string. A weak (W/"…") or multi-value If-None-Match will never match, and you will get a 200 every time.
  • It covers the whole response including count, so any row changing changes the catalogue ETag.

catalogueUpdatedAt is now on every row, derived from the files the payload is actually built from, so it cannot claim a freshness the content does not have.

Two fields are called videoId and they are not the same thing.
The row's videoId is our folder name and is what /checkpoints takes. youtube.videoId is a YouTube id. We nested it rather than flattening it for precisely this reason — join on path, never on videoId.

Everything published from now on carries it. We are not going to backfill something we do not have the records for and pretend otherwise.
