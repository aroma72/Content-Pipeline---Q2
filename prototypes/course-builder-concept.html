What we want to build, and why

Course Builder

Someone types a course topic — and as much or as little else as they feel like — and a complete course comes out the other end: planned, scripted, filmed, and in the LMS. This note explains the idea and what it asks of you. The companion technical document has the contracts.

The problem it solves

Making one lesson video today takes a person: decide the topic, research it, write a script, get the script reviewed, generate the art and the voiceover, render, check the quality, upload, and finally tell the LMS it exists. Most of those steps are already automated individually. What is missing is anything that turns “we should teach X” into a course without a human carrying each lesson through by hand.

So a course that should take an afternoon to specify takes weeks to appear, and courses that would be genuinely useful never get made at all — not because the machine cannot make them, but because nobody has time to shepherd twelve lessons through nine steps each.

The course builder is the missing front end: one box, one button, a whole course.

What happens when someone presses the button

Only the first step is new. Everything after it is the pipeline that already produces the videos in your LMS today.

The one thing to take from this diagram

The course builder is not a new content system. It is a planner bolted to the front of the pipeline that already makes the videos you are embedding questions into. Everything you have already built against — the checkpoint API, the question popup — works unchanged on courses made this way.

What your side has to do

Deliberately small, and deliberately the same shape as the quiz integration you have already built.

  1. A form. One required field — the topic. Three optional ones: duration, audience, and a free-text description. Nothing else. If a user fills in only the topic, the planner chooses sensible defaults and tells you what it assumed.
  2. Call plan, show the result, and wait for a human. Planning takes about a minute and returns the whole course as JSON. Render it and let someone read it. Do not chain straight into building.
  3. Get an explicit approval. Building a course spends real money and hours of machine time. The screen must show the cost and the time before anyone can start it, and our API refuses to start unless your call echoes back the exact number of lessons being approved.
  4. Poll for progress. Videos are produced one at a time. A course is not ready in a request — it is ready over hours. Show a progress list, not a spinner.
  5. Create the course from the plan. You own the course, its modules and its lessons in your database. We hold none of it. As each video finishes you get a YouTube link and its question checkpoint, through the API you already use.

Who owns what

The same division that settled the quiz question: your platform owns the learner and the course; we own the making of the content.

You own

The course, and everyone in it

The course record, module and lesson structure, enrolments, progress, completion, answers, grades, and the learner's identity. We hold no learner data at all and have no database of your courses — a plan we generate is handed to you and forgotten.

We own

Making and hosting the content

Planning, scripting, illustration, voiceover, rendering, quality scoring, the video files, and the in-video question data. You pull what you need; we never write into your system.

Why it is pull, not push

We have no write access to Taleemabad University and are not asking for any. Every integration so far — the videos, the question checkpoints, and now courses — works by us exposing a read API that you call. That keeps your database under your control and means nothing we do can corrupt your data.

What this prototype does not do yet

Stated plainly so nothing here is a surprise later.

Not yetWhat that means for you
No editing of a planA plan is accepted or re-generated, not edited lesson by lesson. If a user wants a different course they change the description and plan again. Editing is the obvious next feature.
Plans are not storedWe generate and return; we keep nothing. If your user closes the tab, the plan is gone. Store the plan on your side when it is shown, or you will lose it.
Build progress is not durableOur queue does not survive a redeploy of our service. A course build interrupted that way has to be restarted. This is a known gap on our side, not yours.
One course at a time, realisticallyVideos render one after another. Two courses started together take twice as long, not the same time.
No images or slides for the course itselfLesson videos are produced; a course thumbnail, blurb art or downloadable material are not.

What we need from you

We needWhy
Your course data modelWhat a course, module and lesson look like in your database, and which fields are required. We shape the plan to fit you rather than asking you to fit us.
Who may press the buttonBuilding a course spends money. Tell us which role in your platform is allowed to, and we will discuss whether the limit belongs on your side, ours, or both.
Where a built course should appearDraft, unpublished, in a particular category? A course that appears live in front of learners the moment the machine finishes is not what anyone wants.
Your appetite for failureIndividual lessons can fail quality checks and get remade. Do you want a course to appear when the first lesson is ready, or only when all of them are?
The honest caveat

This is a prototype, and the planning half is the half that is real: it produces genuinely good course plans today, and you can try it yourself at the link below. The building half runs on a pipeline that works but was built to make one video at a time with a person watching. Running it unattended across a whole course is new, and the first few courses should be watched closely rather than trusted.
