2 · Is a course durable? Now yes — and here is the handoff

You were right to answer this one first, and right that the sentence decided the design. Three things were true, and two of them were worse than the error message said.

The handoff exists. It has existed since 11 September. We never sent it.
The error message cited a document that was written, rendered and then left in a folder. It is attached now as Course-Builder-Technical.pdf — five pages covering plan, build and status, with request and response bodies, the confirmLessons guard, the error table and the limits. You should not have had to ask three times for something that was already finished.

The durability itself is now real rather than disclaimed:

What we foundWhat it does now
The queue lived in the repositoryIt lives in the job store, which relocates onto a Railway volume by itself. A course now survives a redeploy.
Worse: a developer's queue was shipped inside the imageOur build admitted the whole orchestrator directory, so every redeploy did not just wipe production's queue — it replaced it with a build-time snapshot of a laptop, blocked items and all. Two lines of build config; it is excluded now.
A lesson mid-build was never marked as startedIt sat at queued for its whole 30-minute build, so a restart could not tell it from one that had never begun, re-picked it, and the first attempt's spend vanished with no record. It is claimed before anything is paid for.
A restart used to lose the courseOn boot we fold the queue. A lesson caught mid-build is parked as blocked with interrupted: true and waits for a person — approving it rebuilds it rather than publishing a video that was never rendered.

The honest claim is bounded, not absolute. The render working directories are stripped from our image and do not come back, so resuming a half-built lesson is not possible — only restarting that one. A redeploy costs at most the single lesson in flight (about $1.50 and 30 minutes), never the course. Until Aroma attaches the volume, GET /api/v1/health reports courses.durability: "container" and the 404 body tells you the same thing rather than asserting the worst case. It no longer cites a handoff you do not have.

3 · The course API, and the things the handoff does not answer

The attached document covers the request and response shapes. These are the gaps you listed that it genuinely does not cover — answered here so it is one round, not three.

You askedAnswer
Is the plan persisted?No. Hold it yourself and echo it back to build. We keep nothing between plan and build; if your user navigates away, it is gone.
What must confirmLessons match?A count — the number of entries in modules[].lessons[]. Not a hash, not a list. A mismatch returns 409 confirmation_required carrying the real count and the estimate, which is the guard working, not an error.
What is charged, and when?Per lesson, as each is released — never the whole course up front. A course stopped after lesson two has cost two lessons.
Does build accept Idempotency-Key?No. Only /produce does. build is guarded by the confirmLessons echo plus duplicate-slug rejection — a repeated build returns 202 with the duplicates listed in rejected, not a second course. If you would rather have the key, say so; it is small.
Does callbackUrl work for courses?Not yet — it is accepted on POST /demo/make-video only. Courses are poll-only today. Tell us you want it and it is the same signed-webhook path.
Is a reject recoverable?A rejected lesson is terminal and the course stops; nothing after it is built. Lessons already built and approved stay published. Re-planning is how you restart.
Is by attributed per tenant?Yes, the same as /demo/make-video/:id/approve — the tenant comes from your credential, and the name you send rides alongside it.
One you would have found the moment you called it.
Per-lesson approve and reject — the whole mechanism by which a course pauses for a human — threw TypeError on every call. They reached for a queue function that was never exported. Nothing you did wrong, and nothing you could have seen from the outside: the route existed, was documented and was authenticated. It is fixed and covered by tests. You were right not to call a paid endpoint to find out.
